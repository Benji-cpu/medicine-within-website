Subject: the gap between two breaths

Beautiful you,

One more piece of this work before the weekend. Not a new practice this time, but something older: the root beneath a practice you may already carry without knowing where it came from.

Thousands of years ago, in what is now Kashmir, a text called the Vijnana Bhairava Tantra was written down. It holds 112 doorways into presence, described so simply that people are still using them, unchanged, today.

One of the most well known of the 112 is almost embarrassingly small.

After your inhale settles, there is a pause, before the exhale turns. Not a held breath, not something to force. Just the natural stillness that is already there, every single time you breathe, that most of us breathe straight through without ever noticing it is there at all.

Practitioners are taught to rest in that gap, even briefly. It is considered one of the doorways.

Try it, if you like. Breathe in. Let it settle. In that half-second before the exhale begins, just stay. Notice what is there.

That is the whole practice.

If you have spent any time inside Embodied Tantra, this might sound familiar. We work with a version of it we call the straw breath: a slow circulation of your own aliveness through the body, with nowhere it needs to go, no climax to chase, just presence with what is already moving in you. Different container, same doorway. The lineage runs straight back to that same Kashmiri text.

The book many of my students end up studying alongside this work, Lorin Roche's The Radiance Sutras, is a modern telling of those same 112 gateways. After a past Embodied Tantra weekend, several people went home and ordered their own copy. They started half-jokingly calling it "the Bible." I don't think they were entirely joking.

None of this is new. It has been waiting, quietly, in the space between two breaths, for a very long time.

In August, we go so much deeper into breath, presence, and the doorways this lineage points to. But you already have access to one of them, right now, in your own chest.
