# Conversation Gold — off-form voice of customer

> Started 2026-08-11. Companion to `womens-language-bank.md` (women-only, form-sourced) and the form corpus behind `audience_insights`.

## Why people actually join a temple (off-form)

### Community is the product, not the teaching
The single most repeated driver. People check on the room before they check on the content.

- "One of the key things for me with the events is community, so checking in with you — will it be a fun one?" — Jamie, asked unprompted *before* booking
- "Good community?" — Jamie, again before a later booking

**Copy consequence:** sell the room. Who will be there, what the group is like, how it feels to walk in. Not the curriculum. When she dropped the event WhatsApp group for the June edition, a repeat buyer noticed and missed it, and named exactly what it did: coordinating travel, lost and found, and building anticipation before the day. That group is a product feature, not admin.

### The arc is the mechanism
People credit the *sequence*, not the content, for why it works.

- "I've come to two of these now in the farmhouse in Diemen. It gives you a lot of time to get to know people. Ecstatic dance, chatting to people over dinner, then slowly moving into the temple space. It really works." — Jamie

**Copy consequence:** this is the best proof there is for The Art of Embodied Connection™, because AEC's whole job is what he's praising — time to actually meet people before the temple begins. The long Diemen day did it with dance and dinner. AEC does it in two hours.

### They come to find their people, then act on it
- "The way I approach temples is I try to understand the people I connect most with, and then act on those connections. In that sense, this worked perfectly for me." — Jamie

**Copy consequence:** honest and useful, but warm-list only. "Act on those connections" reads differently to a cold audience.

### Mixed experience levels are a feature to them, not a compromise
- "One of the nicest things about a temple experience is that there is transmission across people with varying levels of experience." — Jamie

Notable because he said it while conceding an argument to Sandi, after initially arguing the opposite.

### Energy, and the absence of performance pressure
- "I always get energy from temples. I was scared my rambunctious self would strain my calf, but it hasn't got worse at all. Super happy I came." — Jamie, who attended two days after tearing his calf

**Copy consequence:** rare, concrete evidence that the space adapts to the body in front of it. Most "come as you are" copy has nothing behind it. This does.

### It stays with them
- "Some of the feelings from the temple still very much remain." — Jamie, four days later, mid work trip


### Performance versus embodiment — the differentiator, named by a peer
The clearest articulation of why she wins anywhere in this file, and it comes from another facilitator.

- "So many times when I'm at the temple, I often feel the facilitator is doing a performance. It's almost like in their mind they have this idea 'this is what a Tantra leader is supposed to be' and they are playing that part almost like it's a costume they are wearing. With you it always feels grounded, sincere, and authentic." — Mike

**Copy consequence:** the enemy has a face now, and it isn't an abstraction. It's the facilitator-as-costume. Pairs directly with the brand enemy work in `brand-brain.md`.

### Composure that the room never sees
- "I know, because you told me, that things didn't always go according to plan, and while it may have thrown you off in the moment personally, you handled it really well and no one noticed anything was off. Shows how solid and experienced you are." — Mike

Nothing else in the proof bank evidences this. A peer confirming the room stayed held while things went wrong backstage.

### Connection first, intensity second — the pattern repeats across people
Mike's best moment of Liquid Love came from the DRY space, not the oil:

- "I went to the dry area and had time to truly meet Oksana and connect with her and share some presence and share some affection and establish a really lovely connection. I felt great. And then she and I together did end up going back into the oil and had a lovely time." — Mike

Same shape as Jamie's format quote: establish connection, then go into intensity. Two independent people, two different formats, same finding. This is the strongest recurring pattern in the whole off-form corpus, and it is exactly the argument for The Art of Embodied Connection™.

### A temple is held to a higher bar than a party
- "If this was a similar event at a hotel in Cancun or something, perhaps I'd have had a different standard and been more easy about it and not bothered, but at a temple it's, to me, a different level." — Mike

**Copy consequence:** the temple frame raises expectations. Consent rigour isn't overhead, it's part of what people are paying for. Worth saying out loud in copy.

## The finding that three independent sources agree on (added 2026-08-11)

Mined all 307 Google Form responses (910 substantive answers across 47 forms) and ran term frequency on what people say they want.

**"People" is the single most-used word — ahead of tantra, sex, healing, pleasure or transformation. It ranks #1 for women counted alone and #1 for men counted alone.**

| Corpus | n | Top terms in "what they want" |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone | 277 | people (42), connect (38), explore (36), energy (36), space (36), connection (34) |
| Women only | 43 | people (13), explore (10), experience (9), love (8), open (8) |
| Men only | 67 | people (12), connect (12), space (10), connection (9), explore (9) |

Jamie said it in a DM before booking: *"one of the key things for me with the events is community."* Mike's best moment of Liquid Love came from establishing connection in the dry space first. The form corpus says the same thing at scale. Three independent sources, none of them prompted.

**Copy consequence: sell the room, not the curriculum.** Who will be there, what the group is like, how it feels to walk in. That is what they are buying, and it is measurable, not a hunch.

### Gender coverage, stated honestly
140 of 307 responses (46%) could be gender-resolved. Reliable tiers are self-identification in the person's own words and women-only containers. First-name inference is a weaker tier and can be wrong — the library reads "Sandi" as male. The rest are marked not determinable rather than guessed.

### Three gaps in what the forms collect
1. **She asks what people WANT 277 times and what HURTS only 51 times.** Copy converts on pain, and the corpus is thinnest exactly where it matters most. Add a real pain question to the standard application form.
2. **No form asks gender or pronouns**, so 54% of responses cannot be segmented at all. One optional question fixes this for everything going forward, and the Google Forms API is live to add it.
3. **Only 14 post-event feedback answers exist across every form.** The Integration forms are barely being filled, so the transformation language that makes the best testimonials is not being captured systematically. That is why the best material in this file came from WhatsApp instead.

## Hidden objections, said out loud only in private

### Discretion, for the professional segment
- "I just like to screen for professional acquaintances. Had a bad experience once." — Jamie, on why he wanted the participant list

**Why this matters:** Amsterdam is small. Running into a colleague at a tantra temple is a live fear for anyone with a corporate job, and nobody says it on an application form. Not copy — but a discretion line in the FAQ or the confirmation email would quietly de-risk the buy for an entire segment.

### Facilitation quality is the real filter
- "I think these spaces don't benefit from poor facilitation." — Jamie, deciding to skip another Amsterdam tantra gathering

**Copy consequence:** use the idea, never the quote attributed. It's a swipe at a named competitor's event.

### The positioning problem, from a brand strategist's mouth
- "Because I'm not a beginner? That's for first timers right." / "The communication of it. Isn't it an L1 training?" — Jamie, declining the ET weekend

A warm, repeat, high-intent buyer declining purely on positioning. See the `deeper-intimacy-positioning` memory — the same gap runs the other way too, with first-timers walking into temples more advanced than the ad implies.

## Behavioural patterns worth designing around

- **Late buying, closed by a direct nudge.** One "last male ticket" message closed a booking within two hours. Across a year this buyer hovered and nearly lost his spot three separate times. Confirms the late-buy curve, and confirms the personal DM is what closes.
- **Male-ticket scarcity repeatedly nearly costs sales.** Worth a waitlist that pings men automatically when a spot opens, rather than doing it by hand.
- **The non-transferable ticket policy is a churn moment.** "I guess I just lose the money then." He stayed warm, but he's unusually warm. Corroborated by `field-research.md`, which found Sandi runs the strictest refund policy of any Dutch operator identified.
- **The best feedback is verbal and has to be asked for.** Asked for temple feedback, he first offered to give it in person over wine. The voice note only came because she said she wanted it while it was still fresh in his body. Nobody volunteers this.
- **Loyal buyers drift geographically.** By mid-2026 this one was in Amsterdam 7-10 days a month, between Paris, London and New York. Argues for something not location-bound, or dates published far enough ahead to plan around.
- **No price sensitivity in this segment.** Spa twice a month, Ubers by default, self-described "spoilt expat," never mentioned cost once across a full year. Nothing is currently priced for him.

## Operations findings that came out of gold mining (not copy)

**Liquid Love inverts the consent sequence.** Mike, a peer facilitator, named it precisely: *"Bodies started sliding around and coming into contact... it also meant that for all practical purposes you had to establish clarity after contact had already been made."* In an oiled, blindfolded, free-flow space that is a property of the format, not of the participants. He also reported, in parentheses, that he had to remove someone's hands more than once and the removal was not respected the first or second time.

His fix, which costs nothing: once everyone is naked and oiled, run a couple of **non-blindfolded** connection exercises first, then blindfolds, then free flow.

**Buy a bell.** Same source: talking over a full room on a microphone to signal exercise switches doesn't cut through. A bell does.

**Qualify medicine enquiries on day one.** A seven-week Kambo conversation (three explainer voice notes, roughly six scheduling attempts) ended with *"I may be a bit more interested in Bufo than Kambo."* One question at first contact would have surfaced that immediately.

**⚠️ The spoken Kambo pitch makes medical claims the written copy never would.** Her 9 April 2026 voice note claims a client "was able to get rid of her herpes" and describes work on Lyme. That contradicts her own standing rule (no medical or healing claims, ever), and it's the same category of language behind the Instagram policy risk. The website is disciplined; the voice notes are not. Needs a spoken script for the medicine conversation, not just careful web copy.
