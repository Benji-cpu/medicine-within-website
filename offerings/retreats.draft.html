You are the one who holds it. The job, the household, the people who lean on you, the plans that fall apart unless you catch them. You are good at it. That is part of the problem, because being good at it is exactly why nobody thinks to ask how you are doing.

And somewhere in the middle of all that competence, you stopped being able to find yourself. Not dramatically. You still show up, still deliver, still laugh at dinner. It is quieter than that. You catch it in the pause between one thing and the next, when there is nothing to organise and you realise you do not know what you actually want.

You haven't lost your energy. You've lost the thread back to yourself.

Four days is enough to find it again.

Plainly

What This Actually Is

Four days in a house in the woods of Drenthe, with a small group of women, roughly two hours from Amsterdam. You arrive on Thursday evening and leave on Sunday afternoon.

Your bed is made, your meals are cooked, and every session is planned. For four days you do not organise anything, carry anything, or answer to anyone. That matters more than it sounds. For most women who come here, it is the first time in years.

The days themselves are movement, breath, voice, ritual and rest, with time in the group and time on your own. It is real work and it asks something of you. It is also, for long stretches, deeply restful.

I hold the whole container myself, start to finish. There is no hand-off to an assistant, no rotating team. You are met by the same woman for four days, which means the field stays steady even when what moves through you doesn't.

Before You Wonder

The Things Women Ask Me First

You can come as a beginner

Most women here have never done a retreat like this. Willingness is the only prerequisite. Nothing assumes you have done years of anything.

You can come alone

Most women arrive on their own and leave knowing everyone. You do not need a friend, a partner, or a reason.

Nothing is forced

Nothing is forced. Nothing is expected. Everything is optional. Your yes and your no are both honoured, and your body sets the pace of every practice.

Women in their thirties through their sixties come to this work. There is no right age for it, and there is no version of yourself you need to become first in order to be allowed in.

The Arc

The Shape of Four Days

Every retreat breathes differently, but the arc holds this shape.

We land in the body and in the group. Opening circle, agreements, and the first practices that bring you out of your head and into the room.

Shadow work, archetypal inquiry, and the practices that ask you to meet what you have been avoiding. This is usually the heaviest day. It is meant to be.

The peak ritual night. Fire, voice, movement, devotion. What has been building through the descent finds its expression here.

We slow down. We name what shifted, we build a real plan for carrying it home, and we close the circle with blessing rather than a rush to the car. For those who want support afterwards, integration sessions are there.

What You Take Home

You leave standing closer to your own authority. Boundaries that hold without a paragraph of explanation. Rest that actually rests. A nervous system that stops bracing for a fight that already ended.

Some of it will feel like opening. Some of it will feel like something old leaving. Both are real, and both are the work.

Honestly

Who This Is For

Women who are tired in a way sleep does not fix. Women who have read the books and done the courses and still cannot find their own body in the room. Women who feel the nervousness before they arrive and come anyway.

Solo, partnered, in open relationships, post-divorce, post-crisis, all held equally. New to this work or twenty years in, both belong here.

This is not for you if you are looking for a passive holiday with good food and better yoga, if you cannot commit to the full four days, or if you are in acute crisis without other support already in place. Ceremonial work asks something of you. It is not a rescue, and it sits alongside therapy and medical care rather than standing in for them.
