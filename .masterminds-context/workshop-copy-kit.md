## READ THIS FIRST, plain summary

**The strategic job of this page is one thing: stop selling this workshop as a warm-up act
and start selling it as the front door.** Right now it lives as a paragraph inside temple
listings. It is the only genuinely beginner-safe thing in the catalogue, and it is the only
offer in the business that can be sold to someone who has never heard the word tantra.

Three decisions I made and am reporting rather than asking about:

1. **The hero does not mention tantra, temple, eros, or archetypes.** Every other page in the
   business opens at Level 3 or 4. This one opens at Level 2, in plain language, on the
   feeling. The temple language arrives lower down, after trust. This is the single biggest
   difference between this page and `temple-work.html`, and it is deliberate.
2. **The headline uses their word, not ours.** "Myself" is the #1 want-word across 3,309 form
   answers. The page is built on the forms-gold conclusion verbatim: *they are not buying
   tantra, they are buying a room where they get to be themselves in front of others.*
3. **The 83% boundaries stat gets used as a reframe, not a sales point.** They already think
   they are good at consent, and mostly they are right. So the page never sells "learn
   boundaries." It sells what happens after them.

**The strongest asset on the page is not a headline, it is the exit.** "You can go home at
17:00" is what makes a nervous first-timer press the button, and it is also, paradoxically,
the thing that will sell the most temple tickets. That line does more work than any hook here.

Four things need Sandi before publish, full list at the bottom. The two that matter most:
**the times conflict** (this brief says 15:00 to 17:00; her live Hipsy listings say 14:30 to
16:30) and **the price conflicts** (this brief says €44 flat always, her live October listing

## [Maha 2] THE PROBLEM **[LR]**

### You keep meeting people and never actually meeting anyone

Think about the last three times you were in a room full of new people. A party, a course, a
Sunday drinks thing. You were friendly. You asked good questions. You said your line about
your work, they said theirs, and it was fine.

And you went home exactly as you arrived.

That is not loneliness, not the way people mean it. Most people reading this have a full life.

I ask everyone who applies to my temples what they are actually looking for. I have read a few

Nobody is asking for a technique. They are asking for a room where they get to be themselves

That is what these two hours are.

## [Maha 7] WHAT HAPPENS IN THE TWO HOURS **[LR]** + **[WC]** for the list

### The shape of it

**Landing.** We start with breath, body, and nervous system, because almost everybody arrives
still holding the week. Nothing relational happens until the room has actually settled. This
takes as long as it takes.

**Meeting.** Then we meet, and this is where it gets interesting. Sustained eye contact,
guided, in pairs. What starts out uncomfortable becomes unbearably honest, and then, usually,
tender. Almost everyone laughs at some point. Almost everyone gets a little wet in the eyes at
some point too.

**Moving and playing.** Conscious movement, music, partner practices, and genuine play.
Boundaries and desires spoken out loud, which most adults have somehow never practised.

**Closing.** We close together in ritual. By then something has formed in the room. It always
does.

**What we work with:**
- Landing in the body and settling the nervous system
- Sustained eye contact as a doorway past the friendly version of you
- Movement, music and embodied play
- Saying your boundaries, your desires and your truth out loud, with ease
- The beauty of both the yes and the no
- Meeting people with curiosity and real presence
- Building the kind of room that holds something

**Every exercise is an invitation.** You move at your own pace, in your own way, with your own
warm yes. This space is trauma-aware and held by an experienced team.

> Nothing is forced. Nothing is expected. Everything is optional. Your YES and your NO are
> both honored.

*(Consent block verbatim, fixed unit, never paraphrased. Structure drawn from her own live

## [Maha 19] FAQ **[WC]**

**"What do I wear? I don't own any temple clothes."**
Almost nobody does. Wear comfortable clothes you can move in, the sort of thing you would wear
to a yoga class or to a friend's house on a Sunday. Soft, layers if you like having options.
You stay clothed the whole workshop. You will not be underdressed, and there is no look here
you are failing to achieve.

**"I don't feel good about my body right now."**
Thank you for saying it, and you are not remotely alone in it. People write this to me before
almost every event. Here is what is true: this room is dim, it is warm, the practices are
about attention rather than appearance, and nobody is being looked at in the way you are
dreading. One man came two days after tearing his calf and told me afterwards it had not got
worse at all. The room adapts to the body in front of it. Your body, exactly as it is today,
is dressed correctly.

**"I'm shy in groups, especially at first."**
So is a large fraction of every room I have ever held, including people who have been coming
for years. People tell me the same thing over and over: shy at the start, and then it melts on
its own, they just needed a bit of time. You are allowed to arrive quiet and stay quiet. Nobody
is called on.

**"I've never done anything like this. Is it for beginners?"**
Yes, completely. This is the door I built for exactly this question. Many people walk in with
no prior experience of anything at all. And if you are experienced, the practices go as deep as
you are willing to take them.

**"Is this a sex event?"**
No. You stay clothed, and the practices are about presence, not sex. One person put it better
than I can: *"The session focused on how to make intimate contact between two people more
aligned, deep, and rhythmic. The best part? It was done in a completely safe, respectful, and
non-sexual way."* That is Chris, and that is accurate.

**"What if I can't hold eye contact that long?"**
That discomfort is the practice, not a failure of it. You are never pushed past what is true
for you, and looking away is always allowed. Everybody looks away. That is part of it.

**"Do I need to bring a partner?"**
No. Most people come on their own. Couples come too, and the room holds both equally.

**"Do I have to stay for the temple?"**
No, and I mean that. Plenty of people take the workshop and go home at 17:00 having had
exactly what they came for. Staying is a separate yes.

**"I'm heartbroken. Or just exhausted. Should I wait until I'm in better shape?"**
Come as you are. People arrive here still healing from a break-up, or off the back of weeks
that took everything, more often than they arrive glowing. You do not have to be in a good
season to be in this room. You have to be honest, and willing to look after yourself in it.
That is the whole entry requirement.

**"I just moved to Amsterdam and I don't know anyone."**
Then you already know why this exists. Half of what people come here for is not the practice,
it is the people, and everyone in the room was new once. Amsterdam is warmer than it looks
from the outside.

**"Amsterdam is small. What if someone here knows me professionally?"**
It happens, and it is handled the way it should be: what is in the room stays in the room, and
nobody, me included, will ever confirm to anyone outside who was inside. If this is a live
concern for you, write to me before you book and we will talk it through.

**"Is there food?"**
Not from us. There is a break from 17:00 to 18:00 and no dinner is provided, so bring something
or eat nearby. If you are staying for the temple, eat lightly.

# PART 4: WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS

**Short version: this is the only offer in the catalogue a total stranger can safely buy, and
right now it is a paragraph inside somebody else's listing.**

Everything else Medicine Within sells is written for someone who is already partway in. The
temple pages assume you know what a temple is. The Embodied Tantra page assumes a weekend is a
reasonable ask. The retreat assumes four days and €1,222. There is no shallow end, and the
consequence shows up in the data twice: beginners have left low reviews saying they felt
under-prepared for the open portion of a temple, and warm high-intent buyers have declined
things purely because the positioning told them the wrong story about who it was for.

The workshop already solves this. It has simply never been sold as the solution. It is
**€44, two hours, clothed, structured, taught, and endable at 17:00.** That is the lowest-risk
possible first yes with this brand, and it is the only offer where "I have never done anything
like this" is a normal thing to say rather than a confession.

**So the page has three jobs, in this order:**

1. **Be findable and legible to someone at awareness Level 1 to 2.** This is the one page that
   does not open in temple language. Somebody who has never heard the word tantra should read
   the hero and understand exactly what they would be buying. Every other page can stay as it
   is; this one is the plain-language front door, and it should be the page the ads point at.
2. **Convert nervousness into a purchase by naming the exit.** The three fears that come up
   unprompted and repeatedly in her own forms are what to wear, being shy in a big group, and
   arriving in a bad season. All three are answered on this page in plain words, and the
   ceiling is removed by "you can go home at 17:00." A page that removes the ceiling sells more
   temple tickets than a page that pushes them, which the live 3:2 bundle data already
   demonstrates without any page existing at all.
3. **Feed the ladder.** Workshop at €44 leads to temple at €98 to €147, and the room leads to
   the Living Temple. That is the on-ramp the strategy brief says is missing, except it is not
   missing, it is just unmarketed. This page is the marketing.

**How to position it in one line, internally:** *the workshop is the shallow end, and the
shallow end is the entire reason anybody ever gets in the water.*

**What it must not become:** a second temple page. The moment this page starts describing the
temple's depth, candlelight and eros to win the sale, it stops being a beginner door and the
business is back to having no shallow end. The temple is one paragraph and one button on this
page. Not more.
